A mint for wasted cycles

01 — THE MINT

We started WastedToken Mint in a rented machine shop behind a datacenter, after a retro where somebody totalled up the month's inference spend and the room went very quiet. That number deserved a monument. So we cut a die for it.

Every coin is struck — not cast, not printed. A hardened steel die is engraved in relief, the blank is annealed, and the press does the rest at eighty tons. The raised detail on the obverse is metal that was pushed there, which is why the highlight rakes across it the way it does when you turn the coin in the light.

Each edition is capped, numbered on the reeded edge, and retired once the last coin ships. We do not re-strike. When a die closes we photograph it, log the final count, and shelve it next to the others.

How a coin gets made

03 — PROCESS
01

Cut the die

The emblem is modelled in relief and cut into hardened tool steel on a five-axis mill, then hand-stoned until the fields read as a mirror.

02

Strike twice

Annealed blanks take two hits at eighty tons. The second strike is what pushes metal fully into the deepest recesses.

03

Reed, number, retire

The collar mills 140 ridges into the edge, a laser writes the serial, and when the run completes the die is stamped void.

Provenance

04 — THE LEDGER

Every coin leaves the shop with a printed strike card: edition, serial, die number, date of strike, and the initials of whoever ran the press that morning. It is a shop record, and we keep the matching copy.

Mail us a serial off the rim and we will tell you the exact day it was struck and what the weather was doing, because the log sheet has a column for that and nobody has been brave enough to remove it.
