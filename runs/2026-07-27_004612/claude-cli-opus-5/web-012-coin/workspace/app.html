The Mint

We make the souvenir
for time you'll never get back.

WastedToken Mint started in a two-car garage in Providence with a 20-ton arbor press, a stack of C260 brass blanks, and a bet that people still want to hold the things they're proud of. Nine years later we still cut every die in-house.

Each coin begins as a hand-modelled relief sunk into hardened tool steel, then struck three times under 180 tonnes so the high points come up sharp and the fields stay mirror-flat. Then it's tumbled, antiqued by hand, and the relief is cut back on a lathe until the emblem catches raking light the way it should.

We don't do open editions and we never restrike. When a run is gone it stays gone — the dies are defaced on camera and the plates archived in the public ledger. Scarcity is the point.

From sketch to slam

Four passes, no shortcuts

01

Sculpt

The emblem is modelled at 12× scale, then reduced onto a steel hub. Every letter is drawn by hand.

02

Sink

Hubs are pressed into annealed tool steel, hardened to 60 HRC and polished until the fields read like black glass.

03

Strike

Three strikes at 180 tonnes with an anneal between passes — that's what pulls the relief up crisp instead of soft.

04

Finish

Hand antiquing, a lathe-cut highlight, edge numbering, and a final inspection under raking light. Rejects go back to the melt.
