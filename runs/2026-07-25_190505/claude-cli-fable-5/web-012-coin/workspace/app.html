The Mint

Founded on glorious inefficiency.

WastedToken Mint began as a joke in a group chat and, in the proud tradition of expensive jokes, refused to die. Somewhere between the millionth discarded draft and the billionth burned token, we realized waste had become its own kind of currency — and currency deserves a mint.

Each coin is die-struck in a numbered run, deep-relief embossed, and finished with a reeded edge you can feel in the dark. The obverse carries our monogram; the reverse certifies the exact token count that edition commemorates. No two runs share a die. When an edition sells out, the die is ceremonially retired, photographed, and melted while someone plays taps on a kazoo.

Collectors carry them for luck. Engineers slam them on desks to settle arguments. CEOs keep one in the drawer as a reminder that the burn rate is, at minimum, beautifully embossed. Whatever your reason — if you've ever wasted a token, you've already earned one.
