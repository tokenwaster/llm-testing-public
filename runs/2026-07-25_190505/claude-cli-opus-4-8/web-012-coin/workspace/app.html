The Mint

We turn fleeting tokens into things you can drop on a table.

A screenshot can be forged. A ledger can fork. A coin, struck in metal and pressed with a raised relief you can feel with your thumb, simply is.

WastedToken Mint began as an inside joke about wasted gas fees and grew into a working strike-house. Every coin is die-struck under multiple tonnes of pressure, hand-detailed, edge-reeded, and paired with a serialised certificate. The denomination on the reverse — a token count — is a memorial, not a promise: proof you were there when the tokens were spent.

“Fidelis in aere.” — Faithful, in bronze.

From die to hand

How a WastedToken is made.

01

Cut the die

The emblem is engraved into hardened tool steel — the negative that will emboss every coin in raised relief.

02

Strike & reed

Blanks are pressed under multiple tonnes, then the edge is reeded with 120 ridges and the surfaces hand-finished.

03

Serialise & seal

Each coin is micro-stamped, logged to the vault registry, and boxed with its certified assay card.
