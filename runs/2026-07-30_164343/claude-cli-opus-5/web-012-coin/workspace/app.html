01 — The Mint

A small foundry with an
unreasonable standard.

Two presses, one lathe, four people, and a stubborn refusal to ship a coin that doesn't ring true when you flick it.

✦

Struck, never cast

Casting is fast and soft. We cut hardened tool-steel dies and strike each blank five times at 180 tonnes, so the relief comes up crisp enough to read a serif two millimetres tall.

◈

Finished by hand

Every coin is tumbled, then hand-relieved: fields brought to a mirror while the raised emblem keeps its frosted cameo. It's why the highlight travels across the face instead of sitting on it.

⬡

Numbered for life

The rim carries an incuse serial and the edition mark. We keep the register; if a coin changes hands, the ledger follows it. Dies are defaced when an edition closes.

WastedToken Mint began as a joke on a whiteboard — a tally of everything an engineering floor had burned through in a quarter, drawn as a coin. Somebody cut the die anyway. The first hundred pieces went out in padded envelopes and came back as photographs from twenty-one countries: pinned to monitors, carried in pockets, slid across tables at the end of long arguments.

Ten seasons later the joke has a foundry. We still strike in small, closed editions — never reopened, never restruck, never "remastered". When an edition sells out the dies are scored across the face and retired to the wall. What you hold is the whole run.

Blanks are chosen for weight and voice, not price: a coin should land with authority and hold its edge for a lifetime of nervous thumbing. Everything leaves the bench with a capsule, a case, a certificate on cotton stock, and a rim serial tied to a line in the register.

03 — The Process

Whiteboard to pocket
in eleven weeks.

Nothing is outsourced past the blanking press. We cut, strike, finish, serialise and pack under one roof.

I

Engrave

The emblem is modelled in relief, cut into hardened tool steel on a four-axis engraver, then hand-sharpened at the letter edges.

II

Strike

Annealed blanks take five hits at 180 tonnes, with a re-anneal between the third and fourth so the metal flows rather than tears.

III

Finish

Fields polished to a mirror; the raised relief masked and bead-blasted to a frost. Then plating, if the edition calls for it.

IV

Register

The rim is incused with its serial, the coin capsuled, and its number written into the permanent register in your name.
